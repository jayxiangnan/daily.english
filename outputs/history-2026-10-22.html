The Locked Exit: The Triangle Factory Fire of 1911

1911年三角衬衫厂火灾：九楼被锁住的出口

1911 年的一场工厂火灾，如何让一扇上锁的门成为公共安全改革的焦点？

English Story

01On Saturday, March 25, 1911, workers at the Triangle Waist Company were nearing the end of their day. Their factory occupied the eighth, ninth, and tenth floors of New York City's Asch Building. About 500 people worked there, many of them young immigrant women sewing shirtwaists, a popular kind of blouse. The machines, cutting tables, and piles of cloth made the rooms busy places. To the workers, this was an ordinary workday. Within minutes, however, the arrangement of those rooms and exits would determine who could get out.

02The workers had already challenged their conditions. In 1909, Triangle employees joined a much larger garment workers' strike. After about three months, some demands for better pay and conditions were won, but serious dangers remained. On the ninth floor, for example, a door leading to a stairway was kept locked during working hours. Managers wanted to control movement and check workers' belongings for stolen material. A rule meant to protect company property would soon have a terrible effect on people trying to escape.

03Late that afternoon, fire began among fabric scraps on the eighth floor. The precise ignition source is uncertain; one historical account points to a discarded match or cigarette. Cloth and paper helped the flames spread quickly. Workers on the eighth floor tried to leave, while a telephone warning reached the tenth floor. No comparable warning reached the ninth. There was no alarm system that could alert everyone at once, and the emergency hose did not work. By the time people on the ninth floor understood the danger, smoke and fire had already narrowed their choices.

04One survivor, Max Hochfield, later recalled going down from the ninth floor and seeing the eighth floor in flames. He wanted to return for his sister Esther, who was still above, but a firefighter stopped him. Esther died in the fire. Hochfield's account does not tell us what every worker saw or did; it gives one person's view of a fast-moving disaster. He remembered that no one had warned the ninth floor. His experience also shows how an emergency can separate people before they have time to make a careful plan.

05Some workers escaped by elevator, and people on the tenth floor reached the roof, where neighbors helped them across to another building. On the ninth floor, many found their best stairway blocked by a locked door. The other stairway was threatened by fire. The exterior fire escape collapsed, and firefighters' ladders could not reach the upper floors. These failures did not affect everyone in exactly the same way, but together they made a safe exit impossible for many. Some people jumped or fell from the windows; others died inside from flames or smoke.

06The fire killed 146 workers. That number describes a public disaster, but each death also belonged to a family and a community. The disaster raised an urgent question: why had a modern city allowed a crowded factory to operate without reliable ways to warn and evacuate its workers? Investigators and witnesses focused especially on the locked exit. The factory's owners, Max Blanck and Isaac Harris, were indicted in April 1911. At their December trial, the issue was whether they knew the door was locked during the fire. A jury acquitted them.

07The acquittal did not end the public response. In New York, city and state commissions investigated factory conditions. Reformers, workers, and politicians pressed for stronger protections, and lawmakers passed new rules on fire safety and working conditions. The National Park Service counts thirty-eight new laws in this response. Such changes did not bring the dead back, and laws alone could not make every workplace safe. They did, however, turn specific failures at Triangle—unsafe exits, weak fire protection, and poor oversight—into questions that officials had to address across many workplaces.

08The Asch Building still stands, now called the Brown Building. Its survival can make the story feel distant, as if the danger belonged only to 1911. Yet the central lesson is practical: an exit matters only if people can reach and open it when they need it. Hochfield's memory of a sister he could not save keeps that lesson close to the lives behind the statistics. The Triangle fire became a turning point because people connected those individual losses to the rules, inspections, and decisions that shape an ordinary workday.

中文译文

011911 年 3 月 25 日，星期六，三角衬衫厂的工人们快要结束一天的工作了。工厂占据纽约市阿施大楼的八、九、十层。这里约有 500 人工作，许多是年轻的移民女性，缝制当时流行的一种女式衬衫。机器、裁剪桌和成堆的布料让车间十分忙碌。对工人来说，这原本是个普通的工作日。然而，几分钟之内，房间与出口的布局就将决定谁能逃出去。

02工人们此前已经对工作条件提出过抗议。1909 年，三角衬衫厂的员工参加了一场规模更大的制衣工人罢工。约三个月后，改善工资和工作条件的部分诉求得到满足，但严重的危险仍然存在。例如，九楼通向一处楼梯间的门在工作时间被锁着。管理者想控制人员出入，并检查工人的随身物品，防止布料被偷走。一条旨在保护公司财物的规定，很快就会对试图逃生的人造成可怕后果。

03那天下午接近下班时，八楼的布料碎屑间起了火。确切的引火物尚不确定；一种历史记述认为可能是被丢弃的火柴或香烟。布料和纸张让火势迅速蔓延。八楼工人设法撤离，一通电话则把警讯传到了十楼。九楼没有收到同样的警讯。楼里没有能同时提醒所有人的报警系统，紧急消防水带也无法使用。等九楼的人意识到危险时，浓烟和大火已使他们可选择的逃生路线所剩无几。

04幸存者马克斯·霍克菲尔德后来回忆，他从九楼往下走时，看见八楼已经着火。他想回去找仍在楼上的姐姐埃丝特，却被一名消防员拦住。埃丝特在火灾中遇难。霍克菲尔德的叙述不能说明每位工人当时看见了什么、做了什么；它呈现的是一场迅速恶化的灾难中的个人视角。他记得九楼没有人得到警告。他的经历也说明，紧急情况会在人们来得及仔细制订计划前，就把亲人分开。

05一些工人乘电梯逃生；十楼的人到达屋顶，邻楼的人帮助他们转移到另一栋楼。九楼的许多人发现，通向最可行楼梯间的门锁着；另一处楼梯间则受到火势威胁。室外防火梯坍塌，消防员的云梯也够不到高层。这些故障对每个人的影响并不完全相同，但它们叠加在一起，使许多人无法安全撤离。有些人从窗户跳下或坠落，另一些人在楼内死于火焰或浓烟。

06这场火灾夺走了 146 名工人的生命。这个数字描述的是一场公共灾难，但每一位遇难者也都属于一个家庭和一个社群。灾难提出了一个迫切的问题：为什么一座现代城市会允许拥挤的工厂在缺少可靠的警报和疏散手段的情况下运营？调查人员和证人尤其关注那处被锁住的出口。工厂业主马克斯·布兰克和艾萨克·哈里斯于 1911 年 4 月受到起诉。在同年 12 月的审判中，关键问题是他们是否知道火灾发生时门被锁着。陪审团最终宣判二人无罪。

07无罪判决并没有结束公众的回应。在纽约，市级和州级委员会调查了工厂条件。改革者、工人和政治人物推动更有力的保护措施，立法者通过了有关消防安全和工作条件的新规定。美国国家公园管理局统计，这一应对过程产生了 38 项新法律。这些改变无法让逝者复生，法律本身也无法保证每个工作场所都安全。不过，它们确实把三角衬衫厂的具体失误——不安全的出口、薄弱的消防防护和监管不足——变成了官员必须在众多工作场所处理的问题。

08阿施大楼至今仍在，如今名为布朗大楼。建筑仍然矗立，可能让这段历史显得遥远，仿佛危险只属于 1911 年。然而，其中的核心教训很实际：只有在人们需要时能够到达并打开，出口才真正有用。霍克菲尔德关于自己没能救下姐姐的记忆，让这条教训始终与统计数字背后的人生紧密相连。三角衬衫厂火灾之所以成为转折点，是因为人们把这些个体生命的损失与塑造普通工作日的规则、检查和决策联系了起来。

阅读理解 · Reading check

  1. Why did the ninth-floor workers have less time to escape than workers on the tenth floor?
  2. What did the December 1911 jury have to decide about the factory owners?
  3. Name two safety failures that made escape difficult.

参考答案 · Answers

  1. A warning reached the tenth floor by telephone, but no comparable warning reached the ninth floor.
  2. Whether Blanck and Harris knew that the exit door was locked during the fire.
  3. Possible answers include the locked stairway door, the collapsed fire escape, ladders too short for the upper floors, the absent general alarm, and the unusable emergency hose.
