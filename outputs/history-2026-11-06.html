22,350 Coins: A Wusun Purchase in Han Dunhuang

二万二千三百五十钱：敦煌账目中的乌孙贡物

一笔明确的付款，能说明边地交易的哪些环节？

English Story

01An account from the Han frontier records a payment of 22,350 coins for goods presented by visitors from Wusun. The number is precise; the surviving summary gives far less certainty about the goods themselves. Yesterday's story followed a man from Shache who reported that an item he had handed over was not paid for. This different record lets us ask the next question: what did a completed payment look like when visitors from the Western Regions brought goods into the Hexi Corridor? The answer begins in Dunhuang, where an official purchase could turn a diplomatic offering into a recorded expense.

02Wusun was a polity beyond the western end of the corridor, not a neighborhood of Dunhuang. Its visitors had to cross a long route before they could appear in a Han account. The Xuanquan manuscripts, excavated near Dunhuang, preserve traces of people moving through this frontier system: envoys, escorts, station workers, and officials who supplied or recorded their journeys. A study of the manuscripts by Byung-Joon Kim lists Wusun among several western polities whose visitors appear in grouped travel records. Such lists show contact, but not that every person had the same purpose or followed the same complete itinerary.

03The payment entry appears in an account described by a Gansu archaeology publication on Han frontier posts. Its translated description says Dunhuang authorities paid 22,350 coins to purchase goods brought as tribute by Wusun visitors. We should read the figure as the amount in that recorded transaction, not as a standard price for all Wusun goods. The account does not, in this published summary, identify each object, its quantity, or the people who first produced it. Nor does the number tell us how the visitors divided the payment. A sum written by an office is a window onto a transaction, not a full biography of its participants.

04Why call the goods tribute if money changed hands? Han officials used diplomatic language for objects brought by foreign visitors, but the movement of goods could also involve purchase. The archaeology account explicitly describes the Dunhuang payment as buying what the visitors presented. Kim likewise argues, from other Xuanquan documents, that goods offered as tribute could be priced and handled in frontier commanderies. Those two sources support a careful distinction: a formal label described the relationship to the Han state, while a price could determine what the visitor actually received. Neither source proves that every offering was purchased, or that all visitors had equal power to bargain.

05Dunhuang matters here because it was both a place on the route and an office able to spend and account for money. Jiuquan matters for a different, separately documented case. In Kim's study, envoys from Kangju complained that officials in Jiuquan had valued their camels too low. That earlier dispute does not explain who assessed the Wusun goods or whether the Wusun visitors negotiated. It does show why we must avoid treating 'the frontier' as a single counter. Different offices could handle different items, and an amount recorded in Dunhuang cannot silently be moved to Jiuquan.

06The difference between the two records is also human. In Shiqianjiu's claim, the question was whether a person who had surrendered an object would receive anything for it. In the Wusun account, money was recorded as paid. That does not mean the visitors were satisfied, wealthy, or free from pressure; the document does not report their judgment. It means that an official payment crossed the ledger. For people carrying goods over a difficult road, the point at which an object acquired a recorded value could affect what they took home. For the officials, the written amount made the transfer answerable within an administrative system.

07A receipt-like entry can tempt us to imagine a busy market or a meeting around a table. We have no evidence for either scene in this transaction. The account supports a narrower sequence: Wusun visitors brought goods identified as tribute; Dunhuang authorities purchased them; a payment of 22,350 coins was recorded. The archaeology publication gives that sequence, while Kim's wider study helps explain why diplomatic travel and exchange could overlap. The sources do not establish the date of this purchase in the published summary, the names of the buyers, or a final destination for the goods. Precision about one number must not become false precision about everything else.

08The Wusun payment answers one part of yesterday's question. At least in this instance, a frontier authority in Dunhuang paid for goods carried by western visitors, and the amount survived in writing. It cannot settle the Shache man's missing payment, and it cannot turn the Kangju complaint into a universal rule. Together, these distinct records show that the corridor was a place where people, goods, offices, and claims met. The next inquiry should follow an item whose movement after purchase is documented, if such a record can be found, rather than invent a destination for these Wusun goods.

中文译文

01一份汉代边地账目记载，为乌孙来客呈交的物品支付了二万二千三百五十钱。数字很明确；现存概述对物品本身却远没有这样清楚。昨天的故事写到一位莎车人称自己交出物品后未获偿付。这份不同的记录让我们追问下一步：当西域来客把物品带入河西走廊，一次完成付款的交易是什么样？答案从敦煌开始：官方收购物品，会把一次以贡物名义呈交的行为变成有记录的支出。

02乌孙是河西走廊西端之外的政体，并非敦煌附近的一个地方。它的来客需要走过漫长道路，才会出现在汉朝账目中。敦煌附近出土的悬泉文书，留下了人员经过这一边地体系的痕迹：使者、护送者、驿站人员，以及提供补给或记录行程的官员。金秉骏对悬泉文书的研究，把乌孙列在若干出现在共同旅行记录中的西方政体之中。这些名单说明彼此有接触，却不能证明每个人目的相同，或走完了完全相同的路线。

03这笔付款见于甘肃考古机构介绍汉代边地驿站的一篇文章所述账目。该文的译文说，敦煌官府支付二万二千三百五十钱，购买乌孙来客以贡物名义带来的物品。这个数字应理解为该笔有记录交易的金额，而不是所有乌孙货物的统一价格。在这篇公开概述中，账目没有逐一说明物品、数量，或最初生产它们的人；数字也不能告诉我们来客怎样分配所得。官府写下的一笔钱，让我们看见交易，却不是参与者的完整传记。

04既然付了钱，为什么还称物品为贡物？汉朝官员会用外交语言称呼外国来客带来的东西，但物品流转也可能包含收购。考古文章明确把敦煌这笔支出描述为购买来客呈交之物。金秉骏也依据其他悬泉文书认为，以贡物名义呈交的东西可以在边郡估价和处置。两项资料支持一个谨慎区分：正式称谓描述它与汉朝国家的关系，价格则可能决定来客实际得到什么。这并不能证明每件贡物都被购买，也不能证明所有来客议价能力相同。

05敦煌重要，是因为它既在道路上，也有能够支出并核算钱款的官府。酒泉则属于另一件有独立文书的事件。金秉骏的研究记载，康居使者曾抱怨酒泉官员给骆驼估价过低。那起较早讨论过的争议，不能解释谁给乌孙物品估价，也不能证明乌孙来客曾参与议价；它提醒我们不能把“边地”当作同一个柜台。不同官府可能处理不同物品，敦煌账目中的金额不能悄悄移到酒泉。

06两份记录的差别也关乎人的处境。石千秋的申诉追问的是：交出物品的人究竟能否得到回报。乌孙账目则记下了已支付的钱款。这不意味着来客满意、富裕，或没有承受压力；文书没有留下他们的评价。它只意味着一次官方付款进入了账簿。对于沿艰难道路携带货物的人，物品在何处获得有记录的价值，可能影响他们带回什么。对于官员，写下金额意味着这次转交要进入行政核算。

07一条近似收支凭证的记录，容易让人想象热闹的集市或围桌商议；这笔交易没有证据支持这样的场景。账目支持的顺序更有限：乌孙来客带来被称为贡物的东西；敦煌官府将其购买；二万二千三百五十钱的付款被记录。考古文章给出这一过程，金秉骏更广的研究则帮助解释外交往来与交换为何可能交叠。资料没有在公开概述中确定这笔购买的日期、经办人的姓名或物品最终去向。一个数字精确，不等于其余一切也都明确。

08乌孙付款记录回答了昨天问题的一部分：至少在这一例中，敦煌的边地官府为西方来客携带的物品付了钱，金额还留在文字中。它不能解决那位莎车人未获偿付的问题，也不能把康居使者的抱怨变成普遍规则。这些各不相同的记录合起来表明，走廊是人、物品、官府和申诉相遇之地。下一步应寻找购买后去向也有文书可查的物品；若找不到，就不能替这批乌孙物品编造目的地。

重点表达 · Key expressions

  • goods presented as tribute — 以贡物名义呈交的物品
  • frontier commandery — 边郡
  • assess a price — 估价
  • administrative account — 行政账目

参考答案 · Answers
