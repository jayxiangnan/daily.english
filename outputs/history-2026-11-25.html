When Carriage Gear Wore Out: A Dunhuang Order

车具磨损之后：敦煌郡的一道换领令

太守巡视发现传车用具多有破损；一道命令把修补道路的责任带到了县与郡库之间。

English Story

01A carriage might be present at a Han relay station and still be unready for a journey. In the second year of Shenjue, a report from Dunhuang describes a commandery governor who found much of the equipment for relay carriages damaged while inspecting the counties. His response was an order about replacement and accounting. The previous installment followed a different carriage whose return left six objects missing; the surviving letter does not reveal where they went. This new document cannot solve that case. It asks the next practical question: when an inspection found equipment worn out, how did officials keep the vehicles useful without losing track of public property?

02The order is dated to the third month of Shenjue year two, in the reign of Emperor Xuan of the Western Han. It names Dunhuang governor Kuai, a senior official named Bushi, and an assistant named De. Their message went to the counties and the commandery storehouse. The published slip has gaps, so it cannot give us every word of the instruction. Its surviving sequence is clear enough: after damaged carriage equipment was noticed, counties were to send officials to collect replacement items and deliver the worn items to the storehouse. Both sides were to compare their accounts under the rules.

03That procedure was more demanding than simply replacing a broken strap. Someone in a county had to identify the damaged property, send a messenger, receive usable equipment, and return what could no longer serve its old purpose. The storehouse, in turn, had to know what it issued and what it received. The text does not say how many items were changed on this occasion or how long any carriage waited. It shows a route by which a problem observed on inspection could become a recorded exchange between offices. A road system depended on this work even when no traveller appeared in the document.

04A separate Xuanquan inventory makes the need for inspection easier to see. It lists six relay carriages alongside thirteen kinds of associated objects, including reins, leather fittings, padding and covers. Some readings of the old technical terms remain debated; the list should not be treated as a modern parts manual. Yet it records a basic fact: the number and condition of useful things were not identical to the number of vehicles. In another monthly entry, fourteen bands remained, three damaged and eleven sound. Counting alone would have hidden that difference.

05The same scholarly study identifies monthly records that distinguish objects in the station from objects out with guests. One entry describes equipment that had been sound when issued for passenger service, although it was no longer physically at the station when counted. Other entries distinguish items that were sound, damaged, or repaired. These are separate documents, not a continuous account of the governor’s inspection. Together they explain why his order called for comparison of accounts: a missing object, a worn object, an object away in use, and an object returned to storage were different administrative facts.

06The governor’s order also reveals where authority sat. A station such as Xuanquan served official travellers on the route through Dunhuang, but the replacement instruction addressed counties and the commandery storehouse. The study interprets another short slip to mean that if a relay carriage was beyond repair, its remaining equipment went back to the government storehouse. That is an interpretation of a separate fragment, not proof that any carriage in the inspection order was scrapped. The wider pattern is nevertheless visible: local workers cared for equipment, while the commandery controlled its redistribution and required a reckoning.

07There was a human cost behind those columns of figures. A driver needed workable gear before taking a carriage onward; a clerk or storehouse worker had to make a damaged object legible in a record; officials had to move goods between offices. The slips do not describe their feelings, wages for this task, or the delay faced by a particular envoy. Nor can we infer that every exchange worked smoothly. What they establish is the choice officials made when wear threatened service: do not simply discard an object or ignore the damage; report it, replace it through the storehouse, and check the two sides of the transfer.

08The inspection therefore has a definite result in the surviving evidence: an instruction to exchange damaged gear through the commandery storehouse and reconcile the accounts. We do not possess a matching receipt that proves each county completed the order. The six missing objects in the earlier carriage case remain missing from our knowledge as well. Read together without merging the cases, the records show two distinct pressures on the Hexi road: loss required a search, while wear required replacement. A next question is whether a surviving receipt records one actual delivery of damaged gear to a storehouse and the usable gear issued in return.

中文译文

01西汉神爵二年，一辆传车即使停在驿站，也未必能立即上路。一份敦煌郡文书说，太守巡视属县时发现不少传车被具已经破损，于是下令更换并核账。上一篇追踪的是另一辆传车：归还时少了六件器物，现存公文没有说明它们的去向。新文书不能替那件事结案，却提出下一步实际问题：检查发现车具损坏时，官员怎样让车辆继续可用，同时又管住官物？

02这道命令署于西汉宣帝神爵二年三月，列出敦煌太守快、长史布施和丞德，发往属县及郡库。刊布的简文有缺字，不能还原命令的每一个字；现存内容的次序仍然清楚：发现传车用具多有破损后，各县要派吏员领取替换物，把旧的破损物送到郡库，双方还要依规定核对账目。

03这一套手续远不止换一根坏带子。县里要辨认损敝官物，派人领取可用器物，并把不能照旧使用的物件送回；郡库也要弄清自己发出了什么、收回了什么。文书没有说这一次换了多少件，也没有说哪辆车等了多久。它能证明的是：巡视发现的问题可以变成官署间有记录的换领与交还。即使简文没有出现旅人，道路运转仍离不开这些劳动。

04另一份悬泉置清册让查验的必要性更直观：它列出六乘传车，以及辔、皮革配件、衬垫和车盖等十三类相关器物。若干古代器名的释读仍有讨论，不能把清册当成现代零件说明书。但它说明可用器物的数量与车辆数并不是一回事。另有一份月度记录写着十四件亶带，其中三件破损、十一件完好；只数总件数，就会遮住这个差别。

05同一项学术研究还辨认出按月区分站内器物与随旅客外出的器物的简文。其中一份记录说，有些器物交付接待旅客时完好，但清点时已经不在站内。其他记录分别标注完好、损敝或修缮。这些是不同文书，不能接成太守那次巡视的连续日志；合起来却能解释他为何要求核账：失物、破损物、外借在用之物、送回库中的物件，在管理上不是同一种情况。

06太守命令还显出权力怎样分布。悬泉置这样的驿站接待经过敦煌的公务旅人，但换领命令发给县与郡库。研究者把另一枚短简理解为：传车若彻底毁坏，剩余被具仍须上交官库。这是对另一份残简的解释，不能证明巡视中有哪辆车报废。较大的制度轮廓仍可辨认：基层人员照看器物，郡级机构掌握调配，并要求交代收支。

07数字栏背后是人的劳动。御者要有能用的车具才好继续驾车；令史或库吏要把损坏情况写进账；官员还得让器物在官署之间流转。简文没有记下他们的感受、这项工作的报酬，或某位使者因此耽误多久；也不能据此断言每次换领都顺利。它明确显示的是官府面对磨损时选择的办法：不能随手丢弃，也不能对损坏视而不见，而要上报、从郡库换领，并核对双方交接。

08因此，现存证据给这次巡视留下的明确结果，是一项通过郡库换领损坏被具、并核对账目的命令；我们没有相配的收据来证明各县逐一完成。上一篇传车少掉的六件器物，去向也依旧未知。把两案分开阅读，可以看见河西驿路面对的两种压力：失物要追查，磨损要更换。下一步可找一份真实的收讫记录，看旧具是否送到库中、新具又怎样领出。
