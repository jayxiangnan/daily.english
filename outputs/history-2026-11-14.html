Liuhong Falls Ill: How Xuanquan Examined a Station Horse

骝鸿病了：悬泉置如何查验驿马

一匹有名字的驿马生病后，悬泉置留下了怎样的查验记录？

English Story

01In August 38 BCE, an official at Xuanquan Station reported on a horse called Liuhong. The animal coughed, had discharge from its nose, and was not eating enough. A record gave its color, sex, mark, age, and height before describing the illness. Yesterday's article followed a request to recover the value of missing vehicle fittings, but no linked court reply survives. The horse record is a separate case. It lets us ask a related, answerable question: when an essential animal was in trouble, how could station workers establish what had happened before blame or cost was assigned?

02Xuanquan stood near Dunhuang on the official road through the Hexi Corridor. Horses drew vehicles, carried people, and helped move messages between stations. The station could not replace a sick animal merely by writing a new name on a list. Feed, care, and available working horses all affected the next journey. One surviving record counts 43 horses at Xuanquan: thirteen remained at the station, while thirty were assigned to ten vehicles. That figure describes a particular recorded arrangement, not a permanent staff level. It shows why the illness of one horse was an administrative problem as well as an animal's suffering.

03Liuhong's description helped identify the animal under examination. The surviving text calls it a male horse, nine years old, with a mark on its left side. Its symptoms were recorded in plain terms: coughing, nasal discharge, and reduced eating. The stable assistant submitted the account and named other men who examined the horse. Their conclusion confirmed the reported condition. A museum study presents the slip as an example of checking a sick horse; its wording should not be stretched into proof that we know Liuhong's later fate. The record establishes observation and examination, not recovery or death.

04Other Xuanquan records show what could happen when an animal did die. A researcher from the Gansu Bamboo Slips Museum describes reports that recorded symptoms and treatment, followed by an examination after death. The examiners noted whether the body was intact and whether it bore marks from weapons, wood, or rope. Such observations could help distinguish illness from possible human injury. They were evidence, not a modern veterinary diagnosis. The workers' statements also made the finding accountable to named witnesses. We cannot attach one of these death reports to Liuhong: the sources do not identify it as the same horse.

05The records mattered because the cost of a lost horse could be substantial and responsibility could be shared. A legal-history study cites a Xuanquan slip about two dead transport horses. It states that one horse was to be borne as a loss, valued at 15,000 coins, and divides responsibility among senior staff and an assistant. The damaged, compressed wording does not let us reconstruct every step of that case. Still, it proves that officials could put a value on a loss and specify who was liable. The earlier missing-bridle report asked a court to seek compensation; this separate horse slip shows a recorded allocation of liability.

06Liability did not make feeding or treatment less urgent. The museum researcher notes records of grain and fodder, including a purchase of alfalfa for station horses. Other slips report illness and requests for treatment. A separate order from the Dunhuang governor criticized inadequate feed and late medical care after many horses had died; it called officials to account. These documents belong to different events and cannot be combined into one chain of commands. Read together, they show the range of work behind transport: obtaining feed, noticing symptoms, seeking treatment, examining a death, and, in some cases, settling the loss.

07There is a human cost on both sides of these records. The horse depended on people to notice its condition and provide care; workers could face a heavy debt if an animal was lost. Yet a written name beside a sick or dead horse does not prove that person caused the illness. The distinction between observation, investigation, and judgment matters. Xuanquan's files are unusually rich, but many survive as fragments rather than complete case histories. We can follow the procedure more confidently than any one worker's full experience. That restraint keeps real people and animals visible without inventing a scene or a verdict.

08Liuhong's record ends, for us, with an examination rather than a known outcome. The separate compensation slip takes us further in another case: a loss was valued and responsibility divided. Neither answers whether the county court accepted the bridle claim discussed yesterday. Together, however, they reveal a practical sequence behind the Han road. A station first had to identify its property and observe a problem; officials could then test a report, record a loss, and assign responsibility. The road depended on these small decisions as much as on its geography. A next question is how station accounts tracked the money or animals after a liability was recorded.

中文译文

01公元前38年八月，悬泉置一名官吏报告了一匹名叫骝鸿的马的状况。它咳嗽、流鼻涕，进食也不足。文书先记下毛色、性别、烙印、年龄和身高，才叙述病情。上一篇追踪了要求追偿遗失车具的报告，但没有存世的县廷回文能与它确切相连。骝鸿的记录属于另一件事，却让我们能追问一个相关且有据可查的问题：重要的驿马出事时，驿站人员如何在分派责任或费用之前查明情况？

02悬泉置位于敦煌附近、穿过河西走廊的官道上。马拉车、载人，也协助在驿站之间传递文书。病马不能靠在名册上另写一个名字就被替换。饲料、照料和可用的役马都会影响下一程。一份存世记录写有悬泉传马四十三匹：十三匹留置，三十匹配给十辆传车。这个数字只说明该份记录所见的配置，并非恒定编制。它显示出一匹马患病为何既是动物的痛苦，也是行政上的难题。

03对骝鸿的描述有助于确认接受检查的是哪匹马。存世文字称它为九岁公马，左侧有标记；病状用直接的语言记下：咳嗽、流鼻涕、进食减少。厩佐提交了报告，并写明参与验诊的其他人员。他们的结论确认了所报病情。故宫博物院刊载的一项研究以这枚简说明病马核验；不能把它的文字延伸为我们已知骝鸿后来的命运。文书证实的是观察和验诊，并非康复或死亡。

04其他悬泉文书显示马匹确实死亡时可能如何处理。甘肃简牍博物馆研究人员介绍，报告会记下症状与治疗，死后再行勘验。验者记录尸体是否完整，有没有兵刃、木器或绳索留下的痕迹。这些观察有助于区分疾病与可能的人为伤害；它们是证据，不能等同于现代兽医诊断。有关人员署名作证，也使结论有可追问的见证人。我们不能把其中一份死亡报告接到骝鸿身上：资料没有认定它们涉及同一匹马。

05这些记录之所以重要，是因为损失一匹马可能代价很高，责任也可能由多人分担。一项法律史研究引用一枚悬泉简，涉及两匹死亡的传马。简文说其中一匹计为应负的损失，价值一万五千钱，并在较高级官吏与一名佐吏之间分配责任。文字残缺而简略，无法据此还原案件的每一步。但它确实表明，官府可以为损失定价，并写明谁应负责。上一篇遗失缰具的报告请求县廷追偿；这枚不同的马匹简则留下了责任分配的记录。

06追责并不会让喂养和治疗变得不紧迫。博物馆研究人员指出，文书记录了谷物和草料，包括为驿马购买苜蓿。另一些简牍报告病情并请求医治。一份独立的敦煌太守命令在多匹马死亡后批评饲料不足、医治不及时，并追究官吏责任。这些文书涉及不同事件，不能拼成同一串命令。合起来看，它们展示了交通背后的多种劳动：筹措饲料、发现症状、求医、验尸，以及在某些情况下核算损失。

07这些文书的两端都有切实的代价。马依赖人发现病情并给予照料；如果马匹损失，工作人员可能背上沉重债务。但一个名字出现在病马或死马旁边，并不证明这个人造成了疾病。观察、调查与裁断之间的区别必须保留。悬泉档案格外丰富，却有许多以残简而非完整案卷存世。我们对程序的认识，比对任何一名工作人员全部经历的认识更有把握。如此克制，才能让真实的人和动物留在故事里，而不编造场景或判决。

08对今天的读者来说，骝鸿的记录止于验诊，而没有已知结局。另一枚赔偿简在不同案件中走得更远：它为损失定价并分配责任。两者都不能回答上一篇所述缰具追偿请求是否获县廷采纳。不过，合起来看，它们揭示了汉代驿路背后的实际步骤：驿站先辨认官物、观察问题，官吏随后可以核验报告、登记损失、分派责任。道路的运转既依赖地理条件，也依赖这些细小的决定。下一步可以追问：责任写入文书后，账目如何追踪钱或马匹的去向？
