Two Horses Arrive: Replenishing Xuanquan Station

两匹传马入站：悬泉置如何补足役马

一枚出入簿记下两匹马到站，驿路上的缺额如何补上？

English Story

01A surviving slip from Xuanquan Station records two horses arriving from the Dunhuang commandery governor's office. After their receipt, a dated count gives the station forty-four horses. Yesterday's five dead horses raised a different question: 2,740 coins from the sale of their remains were missing from a cash account. No surviving reply connects that money to a new purchase. Yet the arrival slip lets us ask the next practical question without inventing such a link. When a station lost horses, how could working animals enter its register again? The answer begins with an entry, not with a heroic journey across the desert.

02Xuanquan lay near Dunhuang on the Han route through the Hexi Corridor. Official travelers, vehicles and messages depended on animals kept ready at stations along that road. A horse could become ill, die, or be moved elsewhere, while new horses could arrive. A list of names described individual animals; an entry-and-exit register tracked changes to the total. The dated slip that mentions two incoming horses is complete, according to a published description of the excavated object. It is a narrow piece of wood, but its figures connect a delivery to a station's inventory. The forty-four is a count at that recorded point, not a permanent staffing quota.

03The source says the two horses came from the commandery governor's office. It does not identify them as replacements for any particular dead animals. Nor does it date the slip to the October sale discussed yesterday. That separation matters: a station could receive horses through a government transfer even if a reader cannot follow the money from one damaged account into the next transaction. For the staff, the physical arrival still had an administrative consequence. Animals became part of the station's working stock only when somebody received and counted them. The written total made a later loss or transfer easier to notice.

04Another excavated line is more explicit about a purchase: three incoming station horses, all male, were received after being bought by the commandery. The text establishes a purchase and a receipt; it does not give a price, seller, or proof that the three arrived on the same day as the two in the other record. A scholarly study of Dunhuang's station horses treats such fragments as evidence for several routes by which animals were supplied. Official allocation and purchase were both possible. A neat story in which five deaths were immediately balanced by five arrivals would merge separate slips and is unsupported.

05A third line introduces a different kind of owner. It describes one privately owned male horse, seven years old, marked on the left, with a recorded height of five feet nine inches in Han measures. The stated purpose was to fill a shortage among Xuanquan's station horses. The line gives unusual detail because the animal had to be identifiable, not simply counted as one more horse. But 'privately owned' leaves important questions open. The surviving wording quoted in the studies does not establish whether the owner was paid, how long the horse served, or whether ownership was permanently transferred.

06These entries show a road supported by more than a distant capital's orders. A commandery office could send horses, its officials could buy them, and a privately owned animal could be drawn into station service. The people behind the records included purchasers, handlers, drivers and owners, although these slips do not name them. A borrowed or supplied horse meant available transport for an official traveler, but also feed, care and accountability for the local station. Where an animal came from affected who had to record its arrival and later explain its condition. The records reveal a network of obligations, not an effortless flow of fresh horses.

07The wider inventory gives this small evidence its scale. Researchers describe registers listing coat color, age, sex and care arrangements, as well as changes in the number of animals. Such information could distinguish one horse from another when it was transferred, became sick or left service. A forty-four-horse count is therefore more than a number: it is a snapshot of resources that had to remain usable. Still, the sources do not say that every listed horse was healthy, that all forty-four worked on one day, or that the station had no shortage. A count records possession; it cannot alone measure readiness.

08We can now close the question raised by yesterday's account only in a limited way. The missing 2,740-coin entry has no known resolution, and we cannot call any incoming horse its replacement. What the new slips do prove is how replenishment appeared in writing: a transfer of two, a separate commandery purchase of three, and a documented private horse filling a shortage. The road continued through such small acts of delivery, identification and care. The next question follows the animal after arrival: who fed and worked a newly entered station horse, and what surviving records can show about that daily burden?

中文译文

01一枚出自悬泉置的简，记下敦煌郡太守府送来两匹马；接收之后，一次有日期的清点写明置中共有四十四匹马。上一篇的五匹死马提出了另一个问题：售卖骨肉所得二千七百四十钱不见于钱簿。存世回文无法把这笔钱与新马购买联系起来。不过，这枚接收简仍让我们不靠虚构而追问下一件实事：驿站损失马匹后，役马怎样重新进入簿籍？答案从一笔入账开始，而非沙漠中的英雄旅程。

02悬泉置位于敦煌附近、汉代河西走廊的道路上。沿线官员、车辆和文书依赖各驿站备好的马匹。马可能患病、死亡或调往别处，新马也可能到来。马匹名籍记载单匹马，出入簿则追踪总数的变化。根据出土文物的公开介绍，记载两匹马调入的这枚简上下完整。它虽是狭窄的一片木简，却把一次交接与驿站存马数联系起来。四十四匹是记录当时的数目，不是永久不变的定额。

03资料说两匹马来自郡太守府，却没有说它们是为补哪几匹死马而来，也没有把这枚简系于上一篇所讲的十月售卖。这个界限很重要：即使读者无法从一份残缺的钱簿追踪到下一笔交易，驿站仍可能通过官府调拨接收马匹。对驿站人员而言，马的实物到来同时带来文书上的责任。有人接收并清点，马才进入驿站役用存量；写下的总数也让此后的损耗或调出较容易被察觉。

04另一枚出土简对购买说得更明白：三匹调入的传马都是雄马，由郡府购买后交给驿站。简文证明购入与接收，却没有给出价格、卖主，也不能证明这三匹马与前述两匹在同一天到达。关于敦煌厩置传马的学术研究，把这类零散材料用于分析马匹的不同来源。官府调拨与购买都可能发生。若把五匹死马写成马上由五匹新马补齐，就把互不相属的简强行合并了，证据并不支持。

05第三枚简牵出另一种所有者。它记载一匹私人所有的雄马，七岁，左侧有标记，按汉代尺度高五尺九寸；文书说明它用于补悬泉置传马之缺。这些细节使马能够被辨认，而不只是总数里的一匹。但“私人所有”也留下重要疑问。研究所引的存世文字不能说明马主是否获得报酬、马服役多久，或所有权是否永久转移。

06这些记录显示，道路并非只靠遥远朝廷的一道命令维持。郡府可以送马，郡中官员可以购马，私人所有的马也可能补入驿站役用。记录背后有购买者、照管者、御者和马主，尽管这些简没有写出他们的名字。调来一匹马，官员出行便多一份交通能力；当地驿站也多了饲养、照料和核查的责任。马来自何处，会影响谁登记它的到来，以及日后由谁解释它的状况。简牍显现的是相连的责任，不是新马毫不费力地涌来。

07更广的簿籍背景让这些小记录有了分量。研究者介绍，马匹名籍记有毛色、年龄、性别和饲养情况，出入簿则记录数量变化。马调动、患病或退出役用时，这些信息有助于区分个体。因此，四十四匹不只是一个数字，也是需要维持可用的资源在某一刻的截面。不过，资料没有说在册马匹都健康、四十四匹当天都在工作，或驿站并无缺马。清点能记录存有多少马，单凭它无法衡量实际可用程度。

08对上一篇钱簿提出的问题，我们现在只能作有限的回答：二千七百四十钱漏记一事没有已知结局，不能把任何调入马认作那笔收入所购的补充马。新简所能证实的，是补马如何见于文字：两匹马的一次调拨、另一次三匹马的郡府购买，以及一匹私人马补缺的记录。道路靠这些交接、辨认和照料的小事继续运转。下一问题是马入站之后：谁负责喂养和役使新入籍的传马，存世记录又能告诉我们多少日常负担？
