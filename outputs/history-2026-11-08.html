Why Jiuquan? The Han Offices Behind a Western Road

为什么是酒泉？汉代西行路上的行政节点

骆驼在酒泉估价之后，文书还揭示了哪些沿途分工？

English Story

01Why did a complaint about camels at Jiuquan lead to questions sent to Dunhuang, while the disputed price remained a Jiuquan matter? Yesterday's Xuanquan petition showed that a gift's formal destination did not prove where its animals went. It also left a more concrete question: why did this inland commandery become a place for handling visitors and their goods? The answer begins before the surviving complaint, when Han rule was still taking shape across the Hexi Corridor. It requires us to follow offices and roads rather than imagine a single uninterrupted journey to the emperor.

02The Han state gained control of much of Hexi after campaigns against the Xiongnu in the second century BCE. It then built a new administrative geography. Jiuquan and Dunhuang became two of the corridor's four commanderies, large districts with subordinate counties. But the dates of their establishment are not perfectly settled. Different passages of the History of the Han give different dates, and modern scholars disagree on how to reconcile them. The secure sequence for this story is narrower: Jiuquan's administration preceded Dunhuang's separate commandery, and Dunhuang's territory had earlier fallen under Jiuquan. An exact founding year is unnecessary to understand the later documents.

03This earlier position mattered because westward movement did not begin at the outer gate alone. People, animals, supplies, and instructions had to pass through a chain of settlements and offices. When Dunhuang later became a separate commandery near the western approaches, Jiuquan did not simply disappear from that chain. Historian Byung-Joon Kim argues from the Xuanquan documents that Jiuquan continued to play a central role in dealings with western visitors. That is an interpretation of several records, not a surviving Han decree naming one permanent headquarters for all western affairs.

04One document makes the division of work visible. Envoys of the king of Kangju entered through Yumen Gate and received a pass issued by the Dunhuang administrator directing that they be escorted to Jiuquan. The pass gave them an authorized next destination; it did not show that everyone entering the gate followed the same route. At Dunhuang, officials could register and dispatch visitors. At Jiuquan, another office could take up their case. For the travelers, crossing a boundary meant encountering a new set of officials and papers, even while the journey itself continued.

05The camel complaint from yesterday shows why such a handover could have consequences. The envoys disputed the way their animals were described and priced at Jiuquan. Their claim about missed food, however, generated inquiries reaching Dunhuang, Xiaogu county, and Xuanquan Station. These were different responsibilities attached to different places. The Jiuquan price issue cannot be settled from the surviving Dunhuang replies. Nor should we treat the fact of an inquiry as proof that the envoys' allegations were correct. The paperwork preserves both a protest and the limits of what officials later established.

06Another record gives a different glimpse of Jiuquan's role. Kim notes that the Protector-General Zheng Ji came east to Jiuquan in 66 BCE to report on the western regions and received an imperial order there. The event does not prove that every report from the west stopped at Jiuquan. It does show that an important frontier official could receive a court instruction in the commandery rather than at the capital. Orders, like travelers, could meet their recipients partway. Administrative power along this road was exercised in particular offices, not only at its eastern end.

07Kim also considers the name of the Jiuquan office where the camels were valued, the Kungui office. Its exact function remains uncertain because comparable uses of the term are lacking. He suggests that it may have dealt with people from the west, but this remains a proposal. The uncertainty matters. A puzzling office name cannot by itself tell us who worked there, how prices were calculated, or whether the office controlled every foreign transaction. The stronger evidence is the pattern of documented actions: a pass to Jiuquan, valuation there, and an official receiving a court order there.

08So why Jiuquan? Its early place in Han administration helped make it a durable point of decision as the frontier expanded farther west. The documents show a route divided into tasks: entry and authorization, escort, supplies, valuation, and official correspondence. They do not show a flawless system or a single fate for every visitor and object. The envoys' protest reminds us that such divisions could leave people arguing over food and the value of animals they had brought a long way. The answer to yesterday's unresolved movement is therefore partial but useful: we can explain why a decision might occur at Jiuquan without claiming to know where each camel went afterward. A next question is how the people who maintained these stations were counted and supplied.

中文译文

01为什么一场发生在酒泉的骆驼争议，会引出发往敦煌的调查，而价格问题仍归酒泉处理？昨天的悬泉置申诉表明，贡物在文书上的目的地不能证明动物的实际去向。它还留下一个更具体的问题：为什么这个内陆郡成为接待来客、处理其物品的地点？答案要从现存申诉以前说起，那时汉朝对河西走廊的统治仍在形成。我们需要追踪官署与道路，而不是想象一条直达皇帝所在处、从不中断的旅程。

02公元前二世纪，汉朝在与匈奴的战争后控制了河西大部分地区，随后建立新的行政区划。酒泉和敦煌成为走廊四郡中的两郡；郡是辖有若干县的较大行政区。不过，它们的设立日期并非完全确定。《汉书》不同篇章所记年代有异，现代学者也对如何协调这些记载有不同看法。对本文来说，更稳妥的先后关系是：酒泉的郡级管理早于敦煌单独设郡，敦煌地区此前曾属酒泉。理解后来的文书，并不需要断定一个精确的建郡年份。

03这一较早形成的地位很重要，因为向西的交通并不只从最外侧的关门开始。人、牲畜、给养和命令都要经过一连串聚落与官署。后来，靠近西部通道的敦煌单独设郡，酒泉也并未就此退出这条链条。历史学家金秉骏依据悬泉文书认为，酒泉仍在处理西来人员的事务中发挥重要作用。这是对多份记录的解释，并非现存汉朝诏令把某个永久统管西方事务的总部明确设在酒泉。

04一份文书让这种分工变得可见。康居王的使者从玉门关入境，取得敦煌太守签发的通行文书，指示护送他们前往酒泉。这份文书给出获准前往的下一站，却不能说明所有入关者都走同一条路线。在敦煌，官员可以登记、安排来客继续前行；在酒泉，另一处官署可以接手处理他们的事务。对旅行者而言，跨过行政边界意味着面对新的一组官员和文书，即使旅程本身仍在继续。

05昨天的骆驼申诉说明，这种移交为何会产生实际后果。使者质疑酒泉官员描述和估价动物的方式；他们关于沿途未获食物的说法，则引出了涉及敦煌、效谷县和悬泉置的调查。不同地点承担着不同事务。现存敦煌方面的回文无法裁定酒泉的价格争议；也不能因为启动调查，就认定使者的指控属实。这些文书既保存了抗议，也显示了后续官署能够查明之事的边界。

06另一份记录从不同角度显示酒泉的作用。金秉骏指出，公元前66年，西域都护郑吉东行至酒泉报告西域情况，并在那里接到朝廷命令。此事不能证明所有来自西方的报告都止于酒泉，却说明重要边地官员可以在郡内而非都城接到朝廷指令。命令和旅行者一样，可以在途中与接收者相遇。这条路上的行政权力存在于具体官署，不只存在于道路的东端。

07金秉骏还讨论了酒泉为骆驼估价的“昆归官”。由于缺少可供比对的同名用例，其确切职能仍不清楚。他提出，这个官署可能处理来自西方的人群，但这仍是推测。这种不确定性很重要：一个难以解释的官署名称，本身不能告诉我们谁在那里工作、价格如何计算，或它是否掌管所有涉外交易。较有力的证据是文书所记行动的组合：通行文书指向酒泉，估价发生在酒泉，一名官员也在那里接到朝廷命令。

08那么，为什么是酒泉？它在汉朝早期行政布局中的位置，使它在边疆继续向西扩展后，仍是一个能够作出决定的节点。文书显示，道路上的事务分为入境与核准、护送、给养、估价和官署通信。它们没有描绘一套毫无差错的制度，也没有交代每位来客、每件物品的同一结局。使者的抗议提醒我们，这种分工可能使人们不得不为食物和远道带来的牲畜价值争辩。因此，对昨天未解的物品去向，我们得到的是有限但有用的回答：可以解释决定为何会在酒泉作出，却不能声称知道每匹骆驼此后去了哪里。下一步可追问维持这些驿站运作的人如何登记、获得给养。
