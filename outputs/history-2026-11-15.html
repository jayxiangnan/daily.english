The Missing 2,740 Coins: A Horse Account at Xuanquan

少记的二千七百四十钱：悬泉置死马账目之问

五匹传马病死并售出骨肉，为什么钱簿里找不到这笔收入？

English Story

01A county office compared two records concerning five horses at Xuanquan Station. One recorded the animals as dead and their remains sold for 2,740 coins. The other, a cash account, did not show the money. The surviving bamboo slip asks for an explanation, but its end is broken. Yesterday we saw a sick horse named Liuhong examined and, in a separate case, a loss valued at 15,000 coins. Neither record tells us that anyone paid. This different document takes the story one step further: after a horse died, who checked whether the proceeds reached the public account?

02Xuanquan was a Han government station near Dunhuang, on the road through the Hexi Corridor. Its horses pulled official vehicles and helped carry travelers and messages. Keeping them available required feed, care, replacement animals, and written accounts. The five-horse entry belongs to an October register in the second year of Jianzhao, about 37 BCE. It calls the animals Xuanquan horses and reports that they died of illness. The figure of 2,740 coins is the recorded value of their sold bones and meat together, not the price of five living horses. The slip does not identify the buyers.

03A sale turned the remains of public animals into money that also belonged in an official record. The Xuanquan slip says the cash account did not include the amount and calls for an explanation. This is a revealing interruption in a routine process. Officials were not simply counting dead horses; they compared a horse register with a money register. The wording shows a discrepancy being challenged. It does not show that the money was stolen, that an officer was convicted, or that the omission was later corrected. Because the last characters are damaged, even the exact form of the final question needs care.

04Another Xuanquan entry makes the transaction more concrete without solving the five-horse case. It names a horse called Menghua, records its identifying features, and says it died in December of the second year of Jianzhao. Its bones and meat were sold for 210 coins. This is a different animal and a different entry, two months after the October register. The smaller number is not evidence that one of the earlier five was worth 210 coins. Instead, it shows how an individual horse could be identified, its death dated, and the proceeds of a sale entered in writing.

05A third surviving document shows why accuracy in the sale report mattered. A horse died while being sent toward a military official at Quli. Its remains were sold in Sun An’s household area for 1,400 coins. A station officer then reported that an earlier written account had accidentally dropped the character for “thousand,” making the figure appear as 400. He requested another formal report. The text does not prove that 1,400 coins were physically missing. It records an attempt to repair a written figure whose error could change what the public accounts appeared to contain.

06The three records do not form one continuous case. The five-horse discrepancy, Menghua’s sale, and the corrected 1,400-coin report concern different entries. Together they expose several checks that a transport station needed. A named animal or group was removed from service; death and disposal were written down; the sale produced a sum; the sum had to agree with the money account. A missed entry or mistaken numeral could then travel from a stable record into a county inquiry. People doing the counting and copying had to answer for figures, even though the surviving slips rarely preserve their full explanation.

07Where did replacement horses come from? A Gansu cultural-history account says Xuanquan received animals from commandery stores and could also buy them locally or draw them from nearby stations or counties. A separate study of Dunhuang stations likewise describes horse registers and records of animals entering and leaving service. These sources establish ways to replenish a station’s stock. They do not connect the 2,740 coins from the October sale to the purchase of any named new horse. Treating one transaction as the funding for another would make the story tidier than the evidence permits.

08The unanswered question is therefore precise. We know the county noticed that sale proceeds recorded for five dead horses were absent from a cash account; we do not know the reply or final balance. Liuhong’s examination from yesterday remains another horse’s unfinished story, not a prelude to this sale. Still, the accounts reveal how much work kept a road moving after an animal could no longer work: examination, disposal, recording, comparison, and inquiry. For the station staff, a horse’s death was a practical loss and a public accounting problem. A later installment can follow a documented transfer or purchase of a replacement horse, if its records survive.

中文译文

01效谷县官署核对了悬泉置关于五匹马的两类记录。一份记载马匹病死、骨肉售得二千七百四十钱；另一份钱簿却没有这笔收入。存世简牍要求作出解释，但末尾残缺。上一篇写到病马骝鸿接受查验，也写到另一案件中的损失被估为一万五千钱；两份记录都不能证明有人已付款。这枚不同的简把故事推进一步：马死后，谁来核对卖得的钱是否进入公账？

02悬泉置是敦煌附近、河西走廊道路上的汉代官驿。马匹牵引官车，也协助运送旅人和文书。维持役马供应，需要饲料、照料、补充新马和书面账目。五匹马的记载见于建昭二年、约公元前37年十月的簿籍。它称这些马为悬泉置的马，记下它们病死。二千七百四十钱是五匹马骨肉售出的合计价值，并不是五匹活马的价格。简文没有指明买主。

03出售使官马遗骸变成了也应记录在案的钱。悬泉简说，钱簿没有列入这笔数额，并要求解释。这是日常流程中一个耐人寻味的中断：官吏并非只清点死马，还把马匹簿与钱簿相互核对。简文显示有人追问账目差异，却没有证明钱被侵吞、官吏获罪，或漏记后来得到纠正。由于末尾字迹残损，最后一句问话的确切形式也须谨慎对待。

04另一枚悬泉简让交易变得更具体，却不能解开五匹马那件事。它记下一匹名叫蒙华的马及其识别特征，写明它在建昭二年十二月死亡，骨肉售得二百一十钱。这是另一匹马、另一条记录，比十月的簿籍晚两个月。较小的数额不能证明先前五匹马中的某一匹值二百一十钱；它说明的是，单匹马也可以被辨认、记录死期，并写下出售所得。

05第三份存世文书说明售卖报告为何必须准确。一匹送往渠犁军中官员处的马在途中死亡，其骨肉在孙安所在的里售得一千四百钱。驿站官吏随后报告，先前的文书误漏了“千”字，把数额写成四百钱，于是请求另作正式报告。文书不能证明实物钱款少了一千；它记录的是纠正书面数字的尝试，因为这一错误会改变公账所显示的收入。

06这三份记录不是同一案件的连续卷宗。五匹马的账目差异、蒙华的出售，以及一千四百钱的更正，分别属于不同记载。合起来看，它们揭示了驿站需要的几道核查：某匹马或一组马退出役用，死亡和处置被记下，出售形成款项，款项还须与钱簿相符。漏记一笔或写错一个数字，就可能使马厩记录变成县官署的追问。清点和抄录的人必须对数字负责，尽管存世简牍很少保留他们完整的解释。

07补充的马从哪里来？甘肃的文史资料说，悬泉置的马可由郡库拨给，也可在当地购买，或从邻近驿置、各县调来。另一项关于敦煌厩置的研究同样介绍了传马名籍和马匹出入簿。这些资料说明驿站有哪些补充马匹的渠道，却没有把十月售得的二千七百四十钱与任何一匹有名有姓的新马的购买联系起来。若把一笔收入直接说成另一笔支出的资金，故事虽整齐，却超出了证据。

08因此，悬而未决的问题很具体：我们知道县官署发现五匹死马的售款见于一份记录，却不见于钱簿；我们不知道回文和最终结账结果。上一篇骝鸿的验诊仍是另一匹马尚无结局的故事，不是这次售卖的前奏。不过，账目显示一匹马不能再工作后，道路继续运转仍靠许多劳动：查验、处置、登记、核对和追问。对驿站人员而言，马匹死亡既是实际损失，也是公账问题。若有存世记录，后文可以追踪一匹补充马的调入或购买。

重点表达 · Key expressions

  • cash account — 钱簿
  • sale proceeds — 售卖所得
  • register — 簿籍
  • discrepancy — 账目差异
  • replacement horse — 补充的马

阅读理解 · Reading check

  1. What discrepancy did the county office find?
  2. Why is Menghua not one of the five horses in the October entry?
  3. What did the 1,400-coin document correct?

参考答案 · Answers

  1. The 2,740 coins recorded for five dead horses’ remains were absent from the cash account.
  2. Menghua appears in a separate December entry.
  3. An earlier report omitted the character for “thousand,” making 1,400 appear as 400.
