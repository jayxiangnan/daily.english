# Filling the Gap: Who Supplied Horses to Xuanquan?｜缺马之后：谁为悬泉置补上传马？

河西走廊历史阅读 · 连载 30 · 2026-11-27

一匹马死后，补缺命令会牵动哪些县署与马主？

## English Story

**01** A relay horse could die despite the care of its driver and a horse doctor. Yesterday's account followed Chitu's illness, treatment, and witnessed examination at Xuanquan Station. It did not preserve an order to replace that particular animal. Yet the problem did not end when officials certified a death: a station still needed horses for its next journey. A different document, dated to the third year of the Jian Zhao era, shows Dunhuang Commandery addressing shortages across several counties. It cannot be attached to Chitu's case. Read on its own, however, it reveals how a missing horse could become a question for an entire local administration.

**02** The order was issued in the fifth month of Jian Zhao year three, in the Western Han. It names Dunhuang's governor and senior officials and addresses the counties of Dunhuang, Xiaogu, and Guangzhi. Its surviving wording calls for privately owned horses and government horses to fill shortages among riding horses and relay horses according to an attached list. The list matters, but we should not pretend to know everything it contained from the quoted line alone. What we can see is the direction of command: the commandery did not wait for an individual station to solve a shortage entirely by itself; it drew county offices into the response.

**03** These categories had practical consequences. A relay horse helped pull a vehicle carrying travellers or goods; a riding horse served a different assignment. Calling both simply 'horses' would hide why the order named two kinds of shortage. It also distinguished animals already held by government from horses described as private property. The phrase does not tell us that every private owner sold willingly, what payment was offered, or whether an individual horse changed hands. It does show that officials considered a source of animals beyond their existing public stock when ordinary allocations were insufficient.

**04** For a county office, the order was more than a request to find any available animal. A horse had to be identified, assigned to the right category, and passed through records that could show where it came from and where it went. Other Xuanquan slips describe individual horses by age, sex, height, coat, and marks. A receipt for two horses from the commandery and a separate entry for three males bought by it show actual movements of horses into station accounts. These examples are not dated links in a chain from the Jian Zhao order; they illustrate the kinds of records that could make a transfer visible.

**05** One especially clear fragment records a privately owned seven-year-old male horse, five chi and nine cun high, used to fill a shortage of relay horses at Xuanquan. Its identifying details made the animal more than an anonymous replacement. The entry does not establish who owned it before the transfer, what the owner received, or whether it answered the particular commandery order described above. Nor does it name Chitu. Its value is narrower and stronger: it documents a private-property horse being entered specifically against a station's relay-horse deficit, the sort of action the broader order contemplated.

**06** Putting an order beside a receipt and a shortage entry helps explain the system, but the documents must retain their separate dates and purposes. An order records what officials wanted done; an entry recording a received horse shows something arrived; a horse listed against a deficit shows how that animal was counted. None alone proves that every station regained its full strength. The surviving slips are fragments of working administration, not a continuous diary. That limit is important for the people behind them: a county clerk's instruction, an owner's property, a driver's working animal, and a station's service could all be affected without leaving equal traces.

**07** The wider setting was a demanding road through the Hexi Corridor. Dunhuang's stations supported official travel and transport toward the western regions; a shortage at one point could reduce the capacity available for the next assignment. That consequence follows from the stations' function, not from a surviving complaint by a stranded traveller. Officials therefore had a reason to track both losses and replacements carefully. The records also remind us that keeping a route open relied on many kinds of work: tending animals, reporting their condition, finding replacements, and entering each movement in an account.

**08** The Jian Zhao order ends with a limited conclusion. Dunhuang's officials directed three counties to help fill riding-horse and relay-horse shortages using government and private-property horses. Separate Xuanquan entries show that horses could be received and that one private-property horse was explicitly counted against a relay-horse deficit. We cannot say that these entries fulfilled that order, still less that they replaced Chitu. The next question is what a station did after a newly entered horse became available: which work was it assigned, and how did the register distinguish readiness from actual service?

## 中文译文

**01** 即使有御者照料、马医诊治，传马仍可能死去。上一篇追踪了悬泉置赤兔的患病、治疗和共同勘验，但没有留下专为赤兔补马的命令。官员确认死亡后，问题并未结束：驿站仍需要马匹承担下一程。另一份署于建昭三年的文书显示，敦煌郡要求几个县处理马匹缺额。它不能与赤兔案合为一案；独立来看，却能说明一匹马的缺失怎样成为整个地方行政体系的问题。

**02** 命令发于西汉建昭三年五月，列出敦煌郡太守及其他高级官员，发往敦煌、效谷、广至三县。现存引文要求依照所附清单，调取私财物马和官马，补充骑马、传马的缺额。清单很重要，但不能仅凭这句引文假装知道其中全部内容。可以确定的是命令的方向：郡府没有让某个驿站独自解决缺马问题，而是把县级官署纳入调配。

**03** 这些分类有实际后果。传马承担传车运输旅客或物资的任务；骑马则用于不同的差役。若一概称作“马”，就会掩盖命令为何分别列出两种缺额。文书也区分既有官马与被称为“私财物马”的马匹。这个词并不能告诉我们每位私人马主是否自愿出售、得到什么补偿，或某匹马是否真正易手，却表明官员在现有官马不足时，把官府库存之外的马也纳入考虑。

**04** 对县署而言，命令不只是找来任意一匹马。马匹需要辨认身份、分入适当类别，并通过文书交代来源和去向。其他悬泉简逐匹记录马的年龄、性别、身高、毛色和标记。一份从郡府接收两匹马的记录，以及另一份郡府购买三匹牡马的记录，都显示马匹确实进入驿站账目。这些例子并非建昭三年命令的连续凭证；它们说明马匹调动可以留下怎样的文书痕迹。

**05** 一枚格外清楚的残简记载，一匹七岁的私财物牡马，高五尺九寸，被用来补悬泉置传马之缺。身份特征使它不只是一个无名的补缺数字。此简没有说明此前的主人是谁、主人得到什么，也没有证明它是在执行上述那道郡令；它更没有提到赤兔。它的证据价值较窄，却更扎实：私财物马确曾被具体记入悬泉置的传马缺额，属于那道较宽泛命令所设想的行动类型。

**06** 把命令、接收记录和补缺简并读，可以理解这套制度，但必须保留它们各自的日期与用途。命令记下官员希望办成什么；接收记录说明马匹到达；补缺记录说明一匹马如何入账。任何单一文书都不能证明所有驿站恢复了足额马匹。现存简牍是行政工作的碎片，并非连续日记。这个限度也关乎文书背后的人：县吏的指令、马主的财产、御者的役马和驿站的服务，都可能受到影响，却不会留下同样完整的痕迹。

**07** 更大的背景是一条任务繁重的河西道路。敦煌郡的驿站支持官员出行及通往西域的运输；一处缺马可能削减下一次差遣可用的运力。这是从驿站职能作出的推论，而非来自某位滞留旅客的现存申诉。因此，官员有理由谨慎登记损失与补充。文书也提醒我们，维持道路通行依靠多种劳动：照料马匹、报告状况、寻找补充，并将每一次调动记入账簿。

**08** 建昭三年命令能得出的结论明确而有限：敦煌郡官员要求三县以官马和私财物马补充骑马、传马缺额。另一些悬泉简显示驿站可以接收马匹，一匹私财物马也确被记作补传马之缺。不能断言这些记录完成了那道命令，更不能说它们补的是赤兔。下一步的问题是：新马入账之后，驿站如何安排差役？簿册又如何区分可用的马与已经实际出勤的马？

## 重点表达 · Key expressions

- **fill a shortage**：补足缺额
- **relay horse**：传马
- **private property**：私有财产
- **county office**：县署
- **enter in an account**：登记入账

## 阅读理解 · Reading check

1. Which three counties received the Jian Zhao order?
2. What two sources of horses did the order mention?
3. Why cannot the private horse entry be identified as Chitu’s replacement?

### 参考答案 · Answers

1. Dunhuang, Xiaogu, and Guangzhi.
2. Government horses and privately owned horses.
3. The entry does not name Chitu or link itself to his death report.
