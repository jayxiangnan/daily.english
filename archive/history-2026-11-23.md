# Six Missing Objects: A Carriage Reaches Xuanquan｜少了六件车具：一辆传车抵达悬泉置之后

河西走廊历史阅读 · 连载 27 · 2026-11-23

一辆送使者的传车到了悬泉置，随后的一次清点却让两县官署不得不追问去向。

## English Story

**01** A carriage brought an envoy surnamed Sun to Xuanquan, but a later count found six objects missing from its equipment. A surviving official letter names the people and the discrepancy. In the previous installment, a Xuanquan record assigned thirty horses to ten carriages without telling us where those carriages went. No evidence connects that count to this journey. The letter offers a separate, traceable trip: a carriage reached the station, and its gear became the subject of a demand from another county. How could an arrival turn into a dispute over six objects?

**02** The letter is dated to the second year of Chuyuan, during the Western Han. It was sent by officials of Ming'an County to Xiaogu County and Xuanquan Station. Ming'an's stable assistant Zun had taken one relay carriage, equipped with three sets of horse and carriage gear comprising twenty-six objects, to escort Envoy Sun to Xuanquan. The document does not name the envoy more fully or explain his mission. What it does preserve is a chain of custody. A vehicle and its equipment crossed an administrative boundary, so the people who received and later returned them mattered.

**03** At Xuanquan, an assistant named Chongshi retained the carriage and equipment from driver Xu Yan. Later Chongshi returned the carriage and twenty objects to Xu Yan. Six of the original twenty-six were unaccounted for in that return. The letter does not say that Chongshi stole them, that Xu Yan lost them, or that they had been damaged. Those are different possible explanations, none established by the surviving wording. The important recorded fact is simpler: the number counted at one handover did not match the number returned at another. That gap prompted the next administrative step.

**04** Ming'an copied the list of missing objects into its letter and ordered a clerk to investigate until they were found. The published transcription is incomplete at points, and the summary available in a scholarly study does not identify all six objects for us. It would be misleading to furnish the carriage with an imagined set of straps or covers and call those the missing pieces. The case instead shows why a detailed count was useful. It gave the sending office a specific claim to make after the vehicle crossed into another office's care.

**05** The gear was part of making an official carriage usable. A recent study of Xuanquan slips explains that the Han term beiju referred to sets of equipment for vehicles and horses, while records could also enumerate their individual objects. Other surviving slips report equipment as complete at a handover or note damage and replacement. Those examples do not fill the missing six places in Sun's carriage. They show the broader practice behind this letter: a carriage could be assigned to a journey only if people also tracked the things needed to operate it and checked their condition when responsibility changed hands.

**06** Several people had different roles in this small journey. Zun brought the carriage, Xu Yan drove it, Chongshi held it at Xuanquan, and officials in Ming'an pressed for an answer. The envoy's passage depended on work by all of them, although the letter preserves no account of their feelings or later lives. The shortage created a practical cost: someone had to compare records, write between offices, and search for property that should have come back. We cannot measure that cost in coins, and we cannot assume the carriage stopped working. We can see an administrative effort caused by an incomplete return.

**07** There is an important limit to this story. The letter records a demand to find the missing objects, not the result of that search. No linked receipt in these sources proves that all six were recovered or identifies who was responsible for their absence. The dated carriage trip is likewise separate from the earlier roster of forty-three horses. Joining them would manufacture a continuous log that the surviving records do not provide. Keeping the documents distinct still lets us move forward: after a horse or carriage was allocated, its arrival and handover could create a new question about property and responsibility.

**08** The opening question has a documented answer, though not a tidy ending. A carriage arrived with twenty-six counted objects; only twenty were returned to its driver, and Ming'an asked Xuanquan to find the other six. The letter cannot tell us where they went. It does show that the road was sustained by more than horses moving west or east. Drivers, stable staff, clerks, and county offices had to make each transfer accountable. A next question is whether another surviving handover record shows how officials checked equipment before a carriage set out again. That question needs its own evidence, not an invented ending to this case.

## 中文译文

**01** 一辆传车将孙姓使者送到悬泉置；后来清点车具时，却发现少了六件。一份现存公文记下了相关人员与差额。上一篇提到悬泉置把三十匹马分给十乘传车，却没有说明那些车去了哪里。没有证据将那份清点与这次行程相连。这封公文提供的是另一趟有迹可循的路程：车到站后，车具成了另一县追问的对象。一次抵达为何变成了对六件器物的争议？

**02** 这封公文署于西汉初元二年，由冥安县官员发给效谷县及悬泉置。冥安的厩佐尊带着一乘传车，配有三套车马被具、合计二十六件器物，护送孙姓使者到悬泉。文书没有写出使者的全名，也没有解释他的任务；它留下的是一条物品交接链。车辆及其器物越过行政辖区，谁接收、谁归还便有了实际意义。

**03** 在悬泉，名叫充世的佐吏从御者徐偃手中留下传车及车具。后来充世把车与二十件器物交还徐偃；与原来的二十六件相比，归还时少了六件。公文没有说充世偷走了它们，也没有说徐偃弄丢了它们，或器物已损坏。这些都是不同的可能解释，现存文字没有证实任何一种。记录明确的事实更简单：两次交接的数字对不上；这个差额引出了下一步公文处理。

**04** 冥安在公文中抄录了所缺器物的清单，并要求令史追查，务必找回。刊布的释文有残缺；一项学术研究的梳理也不足以让我们逐一确定这六件器物。若凭想象列出缰带或车盖，再说它们就是所缺之物，便会误导读者。这件事真正说明了细致清点的用途：车辆进入另一官署保管后，发出方仍能提出明确的追索要求。

**05** 被具关系到官用传车能否使用。近期一项悬泉简研究指出，汉代“被具”指车马成套用具，文书也会逐件清点。另有简文在交接时注明器物完好，或记录损敝与更换。这些例子不能替孙姓使者所乘传车补出那六件缺物；它们说明这封公文背后的通常做法：传车可以派往一趟行程，负责人员还必须登记使它得以运行的物件，并在责任转移时检查其状态。

**06** 这段短途行程涉及分工不同的几个人：尊带来传车，徐偃驾车，充世在悬泉留管车具，冥安的官员则要求交代。使者能继续行路，有赖于这些人的劳动，尽管公文没有留下他们的感受或此后的生活。少还器物带来了实际代价：有人必须核对记录、在官署间行文、寻找本应归还的财物。我们无法把这项代价折算成钱，也不能断定车从此不能使用；能看见的是一次不完整归还引发的追查。

**07** 这件事有一处重要边界：公文记录的是追寻缺物的要求，并非追查结果。这些资料中没有相连的收讫文书证明六件器物全部找回，也不能据此确定谁该为缺失负责。这趟有日期的传车行程同样独立于上一篇的四十三匹马清册；若把两者接成一份连续日志，就是制造史料没有提供的联系。保持文书彼此独立，历史仍能向前走一步：车马分配之后，抵达与交接又会产生财物和责任的新问题。

**08** 开篇的问题有据可答，却没有圆满结局：传车到来时清点了二十六件器物，归还御者时只有二十件，冥安于是要求悬泉追寻其余六件。公文没有告诉我们它们去了哪里，却显示道路的维持不只是马匹向东或向西奔走。御者、厩吏、令史和县署都要让每次交接有账可查。下一步可以追问：是否有另一份现存交接记录，说明传车再次出发前怎样核查器物？这个问题需要自己的证据，不能用想象为本案补结尾。

## 重点表达 · Key expressions

- **unaccounted for**：去向不明
- **surviving record**：现存记录

## 阅读理解 · Reading check

1. How many objects were counted before the carriage journey, and how many were returned?
2. Why did Ming'an contact Xuanquan?
3. What outcome does the surviving letter not establish?

### 参考答案 · Answers

1. Twenty-six were counted; twenty were returned.
2. It wanted a clerk to investigate and find the six missing objects.
3. It does not establish where the objects went, whether they were recovered, or who was responsible.
