# The Missing Bridles: An Account from Xuanquan Station｜遗失的缰具：悬泉置如何上报官物损失

河西走廊历史阅读 · 连载 21 · 2026-11-13

一份悬泉置报告把失物、责任人与追偿请求送到了县廷；后续结局有记录吗？

## English Story

**01** A letter arriving at a Han frontier office did not show what happened after it was opened. Yesterday we followed seals and arrival marks to that desk; today a different kind of record takes us one step further. At Xuanquan Station, near Dunhuang, an official reported equipment lost from the vehicles used on the road. His report asked the county court to examine the loss and order compensation. We can see a proposed response to a practical problem, although the surviving report does not show the court's final decision. That gap is as important as the request itself.

**02** Xuanquan was a station on the route through the Hexi Corridor. Horses, vehicles, and their fittings had to be available for official travel. A vehicle could still stand in the yard while a missing bridle or worn fitting made it harder to use. A recent study of excavated Xuanquan slips describes the equipment as state property supplied and managed through official stores. Lists counted not only vehicles but also their separate parts. The purpose was practical: workers needed to know what was usable, what required replacement, and what had disappeared while people carried out their duties.

**03** One dated slip brings the problem into focus. In February of the first year of Yangshuo, 24 BCE, the stable official at Xuanquan sent a report to the county court. He listed vehicle equipment lost by officials and drivers over the preceding three years. The surviving words ask the court to examine the attached register and demand compensation from those responsible. This was not a vague complaint that the road was poorly supplied. It named a period, a category of missing property, and the office expected to act. The attachment itself is not a complete surviving account of every individual loss.

**04** Other slips show why a register mattered. One records a missing leather bridle valued at 600 coins and names the officials associated with responsibility: Guo Ding, Zhao Xin, and Xi Yin. Another records an assistant, Zhang Cheng, and a missing bridle valued at 500 coins. These are separate documents; we cannot assume that each item belonged to the February report or that each person worked on the same day. Their value lies in showing the kind of information an inquiry needed: an object, a price, and a person whose responsibility could be examined. A number on wood could become a real financial burden.

**05** Responsibility was not always simple. An object might pass between a storehouse, a station official, and a driver before anyone noticed its loss. A further fragment names a person responsible for equipment and a driver who knew about a transfer, but it breaks before the full outcome appears. The modern researchers therefore make cautious judgments about some job titles and duties. They suggest that Guo Ding may have held both station and stable responsibilities; the surviving titles alone do not settle that point. Naming people in a record made an inquiry possible, but a name beside a loss does not by itself prove negligence.

**06** The station had another route for damaged equipment. The study identifies slips in which a commandery store instructed a station to send worn fittings back and collect replacements. This distinguishes wear from disappearance. A broken part could be returned through an official exchange; a missing part called for an account of where it went. Both procedures cost labor and time. The people maintaining the road were not simply waiting for travelers. They were counting property, moving it between offices, and answering for losses while trying to keep transport ready. That administrative work supported every official journey through the corridor.

**07** A museum researcher describes a separate order from the Dunhuang governor concerning poor care of station horses. It faulted inadequate feeding and delayed treatment, and identified officials for legal responsibility after numerous deaths. That order shows a higher office could intervene in station management. It is not a reply to the February equipment report, and the two cases must not be joined into one investigation. Together they reveal a system in which problems could travel upward as reports and instructions could travel downward. The cost of failure fell on animals first and could then fall on the workers charged with their care.

**08** The February report thus answers a narrower question than an imagined completed case would. A Xuanquan official noticed losses, gathered records, and asked the county court to pursue compensation; surviving fragments also show how objects and people could be identified. We do not know whether the court accepted every claim, who finally paid, or whether the missing fittings were recovered. Those limits leave the central result intact: after a letter was opened, frontier administration could move from information to a documented request for action. To learn what action followed, we would need the court's reply or a later account tied securely to this report.

## 中文译文

**01** 一封文书送到汉代边塞官署，并不能说明启封后发生了什么。上一篇沿印章和到达记号追到案头；今天，另一类记录让我们再往前走一步。在敦煌附近的悬泉置，一名官吏上报了驿路车辆器具的遗失。他请求县廷核查损失并下令赔偿。我们能看见针对实际问题提出的处理办法，但存世报告没有显示县廷最终如何裁决。这个空白与请求本身同样重要。

**02** 悬泉置是河西走廊驿路上的一处站点。官员公务出行需要马匹、车辆和配套器具。车即使还在，缺少缰具或器件磨损，也会影响使用。近期一项研究根据悬泉出土简牍指出，这些器具属于官物，由官府库藏供应并管理。簿册不仅清点车辆，也逐件记录配件。其目的很实际：工作人员要知道什么还能用、什么需要更换，以及什么在履行公务时遗失。

**03** 一枚有纪年的简让问题变得具体。阳朔元年，即公元前24年二月，悬泉的厩啬夫向县廷递交报告，列出此前三年吏员和御者遗失的车辆器具。存世文字请求县廷核查随附清单，并向责任人追偿。这不是泛泛抱怨驿路物资不足，而是点明了时间范围、遗失官物的类别，以及应当处理的机关。不过，所附清单并未完整保存每一项个人损失。

**04** 其他简牍说明清单为何重要。一枚简记下遗失的皮革缰具，估值六百钱，并列出与责任有关的郭定、赵欣、希音；另一枚记载佐吏张成遗失一副价值五百钱的缰具。这是不同文书，不能假定每件器物都列在二月的报告中，也不能让这些人都在同一天当值。它们的价值在于展示核查所需的信息：器物、价钱，以及需要厘清责任的人。木简上的数字可能成为真实的经济负担。

**05** 责任并不总是简单明了。器物可能在库房、驿站官吏与御者之间多次移交，遗失才被发现。另一枚残简写到管理器具的人和知晓交接的御者，却在完整结果出现前断裂。因此，现代研究者对部分职务和职责作出审慎判断。他们推测郭定或兼管置务和厩务；仅凭存世职称仍不能定论。在记录中写明人名，使调查成为可能，但名字与遗失并列，本身并不能证明疏忽。

**06** 对于损坏的器具，驿站还有另一条处理路径。研究指出，有简牍记载郡库通知驿站送回旧损配件并领取替换件。这就把磨损与遗失区分开来：损坏器物可以依官府流程交旧换新，失踪器物则需要说明去向。两种手续都耗费人力和时间。维护道路的人并非只在等待旅客；他们清点官物、在机关之间移交，并在维持交通运转的同时对损失作出交代。这些行政劳动支撑着经过走廊的每一次公务行程。

**07** 甘肃简牍博物馆研究人员还介绍了另一份敦煌太守针对驿马照料不善的命令。文书指责喂养不足、治疗不及时，并在大量马匹死亡后追究有关官吏的法律责任。这说明上级官署能够介入驿站管理。但它并非二月器具报告的回文，不能把两件事合并成同一场调查。并观两案，可以看到问题通过报告上行、指令由上级下达的制度。失职的代价先由马匹承受，也可能落到负责照料它们的人身上。

**08** 因此，二月的报告回答的是一个比想象中的完整结案更窄的问题：悬泉官吏发现损失、汇集记录，并请求县廷追偿；存世残简还显示器物与经手人可以怎样被辨认。县廷是否全部采纳、最终由谁付款、遗失器具是否找回，我们都不知道。这些边界不影响核心结论：文书启封后，边塞行政可以从获知问题推进到有记录的处置请求。若要知道随后采取了什么行动，仍需找到与这份报告确切相连的县廷回文或后续账目。
