# Four Notices at Yuli: A Handover on the Han Road｜鱼离交接的四份文书：汉代驿路上的责任

河西走廊历史阅读 · 连载 18 · 2026-11-10

一枚写着两个人名的木简，怎样留下文书交接的证据？

## English Story

**01** A wooden slip from Xuanquan Station records a small handover with unusual precision. Four official notices were sent east under the Dunhuang governor's seal. At a time described as late in the day, a driver named Fang from the Xuanquan stable handed them to a soldier named Xi at Yuli Station. The month is missing from the surviving date. Yesterday's story followed millet arriving at Xuanquan and asked who did the work that kept the station open. This slip follows a different duty: how did an important message pass from one worker to another without losing responsibility along the road?

**02** Xuanquan was a government relay station in the Dunhuang area of the Hexi Corridor. Its horses, vehicles, rooms, stores, and staff supported authorized journeys as well as written orders. A command from an office could matter far away, but distance created a practical problem. The same person could not simply carry every document from its starting point to every destination. Stations and their workers made a chain of transfers. Each link needed to know what had arrived, what should leave, and who accepted it. The named handover at Yuli lets us see one link, rather than imagine an effortless journey across a map.

**03** The slip lists four destinations: the postal inspector, Guangzhi, Ming'an, and Yuanquan. They were not four copies with an unspecified common endpoint. The record distinguishes the intended recipients or places, identifies the governor's seal, and names the two people at the transfer. It also gives a day and a time, although the missing month prevents a complete modern date. These are administrative details, yet they answer a human question. If an item failed to arrive, officials needed a way to ask where it had last been held. The entry documents a handover; it does not prove that each notice reached its final destination.

**04** The surviving object adds another layer of control. It is a notched tally: matching cuts allowed two parts to be compared. A museum account explains that one part could remain at Xuanquan while the receiving side held the other. The cuts and written time helped establish that the paired parts belonged to the same transaction. We should not turn that explanation into a claim that the complete journey of these four notices can now be reconstructed. The value of the tally was narrower and more immediate. At a boundary between workers, it made a transfer open to checking later.

**05** Another set of slips names a different station worker, Hong, the officer in charge at Xuanquan. One document from 63 BCE has him submit a register of iron tools. Other records associate him with food distributions and transport paperwork. These examples show that station management involved more than greeting travelers: equipment, supplies, documents, and people all entered its written routine. Hong's records belong to another period from the handover involving Fang and Xi. They cannot place the three men on one shift or prove that Hong personally ordered those four notices east. Their connection is institutional, not a meeting we can narrate.

**06** The station's staff was not one kind of worker. A museum lecture describes a recorded establishment of 37 people, including officials, soldiers, laborers, and drivers, under a station head. Another scholarly account notes that a different slip gives 47, so neither number should be presented as a permanent headcount for every year. Titles for assistants and specialists appear in the documents. Yet titles do not tell us how every person experienced a day of work. Some names survive because a transaction required them; many others who fed horses, kept stores, or carried supplies remain difficult to identify.

**07** The stakes of this routine were larger than four pieces of writing. Messages about government business, travel, or urgent affairs had to move through a corridor where offices were separated by substantial distances. A record of sender, receiver, seal, destination, and time created a trail of accountability. It also put pressure on workers who could be judged for delay, loss, or damaged seals, according to the museum's account of postal checks. The slip does not tell us whether Fang or Xi was ever praised or punished. It shows why a handover was recorded so carefully: the next station needed both the documents and a defensible account of receiving them.

**08** The millet delivery showed how material supplies reached Xuanquan; this named handover shows how responsibility moved outward from it. Neither slip describes the whole corridor, and neither worker should stand for every community that used the road. But together they replace a vague image of a busy route with two verifiable acts: grain received and documents passed on. At Yuli, the record's conclusion is a completed transfer, not a known arrival at all four destinations. A further question follows naturally: what do surviving records reveal when a message was late or lost, and how did an inquiry identify the responsible link?

## 中文译文

**01** 一枚悬泉置木简把一次小小的交接记得格外具体：四份盖有敦煌太守印章的官府文书向东发出。在记作当日较晚的时刻，悬泉厩的车夫放将文书交给鱼离置的士卒憙。现存日期缺了月份。昨天的文章追踪运抵悬泉置的粟，并追问是谁维持驿站运作。这枚简牍转向另一项职务：重要文书沿路交到下一位工作人员手中时，责任怎样不随之丢失？

**02** 悬泉置是河西走廊敦煌地区的一处官办驿站。马匹、车辆、房舍、储备和人员，既服务获准出行者，也支持政令文书传递。一道官府命令可能关系远方，但距离带来实际难题：不可能总由同一人把每份文书从起点送到每个终点。驿站和工作人员组成交接链，每一环都需要知道收到了什么、应当发出什么、由谁接收。鱼离的具名交接让我们看见其中一环，而不必把地图上的远行想得毫不费力。

**03** 简牍列出四个去向：督邮、广至、冥安和渊泉。这不是终点不明的四份副本。记录区分收件官员或地点，注明太守印章，并写出交接双方的姓名；它还记下日与时，但缺失的月份使我们无法换算出完整的现代日期。这些行政细节回答了一个关乎人的问题：若文书未能送达，官员需要知道它最后由谁经手。这条记录证明发生了交接，却不能证明四份文书都抵达最终目的地。

**04** 现存实物还显示另一层核验办法：这是一枚带刻齿的简牍，两部分的齿痕可相互比对。博物馆介绍说，一半可留在悬泉置，另一半由接收方持有。齿痕和写下的时间，有助于证明两半属于同一次交接。不能因此声称四份文书的全程如今都能复原。刻齿简的作用更具体，也更切近：在两名工作人员的责任交界处，让这次移交日后能够核查。

**05** 另一组简牍记下另一位工作人员：悬泉置负责人弘。一份公元前63年的文书记载他呈送铁器簿；其他记录还将他与发放食物和传递文书的事务联系起来。这些例子表明，管理驿站不止是接待旅客，器具、给养、文书和人员都进入书面日常。弘的记录与放、憙交接的记录并非同一时期，不能把三人安排在同一个班次，更不能断言弘亲自下令发送那四份文书。他们的联系属于制度，而非可以叙述的一次相遇。

**06** 驿站人员并非同一种身份。中国丝绸博物馆的一场讲座介绍，一份编制记录列有37名官员、士卒、劳作者与车夫，由置啬夫统领；另一项研究指出，还有简牍记为47人。因此，不能把其中任何一个数字当作所有年份不变的在岗人数。文书也留下助手和专职人员的职名，但职名不能告诉我们每个人一天的全部经历。有些姓名因一笔交接而存留；喂马、管仓、搬运给养的许多人仍难以辨认。

**07** 这套日常事务的分量，不止于四份文字材料。政务、旅途和紧急事项的文书，需要经过官署相隔甚远的走廊。发出者、接收者、印章、去向与时间形成可追查的责任线索。博物馆对邮书考核的介绍也表明，延误、遗失或封泥损坏可能使经手人员被追责。这枚简没有告诉我们放或憙是否曾受奖惩，却说明交接为何记得如此仔细：下一站既要拿到文书，也要留下经得起核对的接收记录。

**08** 昨天的运粟记录说明物资如何到达悬泉置；今天的具名交接则说明责任如何从这里继续向外传递。两枚简牍都不能代表整条走廊，任何一位工作人员也不能代表所有使用道路的社群。但它们合起来，用两件可核实的行动代替了“道路繁忙”的模糊印象：粮食收到了，文书交出去了。鱼离这条记录的结论是移交完成，而非四份文书都已抵达终点。下一步自然要问：若邮书延误或遗失，现存记录能否显示调查如何找到负责的那一环？

## 重点表达 · Key expressions

- **relay station**：驿站
- **handover**：交接
- **notched tally**：刻齿简
- **chain of transfers**：交接链
- **accountability**：可追查的责任

## 阅读理解 · Reading check

1. What does the Yuli slip prove about the four notices?
2. Why should Hong not be placed on the same shift as Fang and Xi?
3. What could the notches help workers check?

### 参考答案 · Answers

1. It records a handover from Fang to Xi, not final delivery to all four destinations.
2. His cited records belong to another period; no source establishes such a meeting.
3. That two parts of a tally belonged to the same handover.
