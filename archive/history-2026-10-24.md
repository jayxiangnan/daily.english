# The Berlin Airlift: Why the Western Allies Chose Planes over Withdrawal｜柏林空运：西方盟国为何选择飞机而非撤离

每日英文历史 · 2026-10-24

一座被切断陆路的城市，迫使昔日盟友重新衡量战争、撤离与坚守的代价。

## English Story

**01** On 24 June 1948, road, rail, and canal routes from western Germany into the western sectors of Berlin were blocked. Food and coal could no longer arrive by the usual routes. The city lay deep inside the Soviet occupation zone, so this was more than a transport problem: leaving would weaken the Western Allies' position in Germany, while forcing a way through on the ground could start a war. They chose a third course. Aircraft would carry supplies over the blockade, day after day. Could an entire urban population be sustained through the air?

**02** The crisis grew out of the unsettled end of the Second World War. In 1945, the United States, Britain, France, and the Soviet Union occupied separate zones of Germany; they also divided Berlin into four sectors, although the city stood within the Soviet zone. The powers had agreed on occupation, but not on a lasting future for Germany. As cooperation gave way to rivalry, the western governments moved toward rebuilding their zones together and forming a West German state. Soviet leaders opposed that direction. Berlin, governed by competing powers in one city, became the place where their plans collided.

**03** Money brought the dispute to a head. In June 1948, the Western Allies introduced a new currency in their German zones; a separate contest over currency followed in Berlin. The Soviet authorities tried to extend their currency reform across the whole city. After that failed, they closed the land and water links to its western sectors on 24 June. Historians identify currency reform as an immediate trigger, but the blockade belonged to a wider struggle over who would shape Germany's future. It also cut normal deliveries of electricity and essential supplies, making pressure on the western sectors immediate rather than merely symbolic.

**04** The Western Allies still had access through agreed air corridors. Their military presence in Berlin was much smaller than Soviet forces nearby, and a land convoy might have led to armed confrontation. An airlift offered a way to remain without immediately testing the blockade with ground troops. It was a political choice as well as a logistical gamble: planes could bring help to Berliners, but only if flights arrived often enough to replace trains and barges. On 26 June, the United States began its air operation; British flights followed. Neither side could know at the outset how long the test would last.

**05** Flying in a few emergency loads was possible; feeding and heating a city for months was another matter. Aircraft had to carry flour, other food, and especially coal, which was heavy but essential for power and warmth. Berlin's airfields and unloading crews had to handle a growing stream of flights. The operation expanded and became more efficient rather than ending after an initial show of resolve. Even so, an airlift could not restore ordinary city life. Residents lived with shortages, and the approach of winter made reliable fuel deliveries as important as food.

**06** Berliners were not simply spectators in a contest between governments. Their continued presence, work at the airfields, and public resistance to Soviet pressure affected what the airlift meant politically. In September, a large crowd gathered near the Reichstag to oppose Soviet domination and press the Western powers to stay. The demonstration did not by itself decide the crisis; the flights, the broader diplomacy, and pressure on both sides also mattered. But it showed that keeping western Berlin supplied was tied to the choices of people who lived there, not only to aircraft and diplomatic maps.

**07** By spring 1949, the sustained flow of supplies had made a quick collapse of the western sectors less likely. Western restrictions on trade with the Soviet zone also increased pressure there. The four powers agreed to remove their transport and trade restrictions, and the Soviet blockade ended on 12 May 1949. The airlift continued until September, allowing supplies to be built up in case access was cut again. Ending the blockade did not settle the original argument over Germany. Later that month, the Federal Republic of Germany was established in the west; a separate state followed in the east.

**08** The Berlin Airlift succeeded because a geographical weakness became a practical challenge that the Western Allies could meet, though at great cost and effort. Air corridors allowed them to avoid the immediate dangers of a ground confrontation, while repeated deliveries made the decision to stay credible. The blockade failed to drive them out, yet it hardened a division that neither planes nor negotiations could quickly undo. Berlin remained a divided city. The episode shows how a decision made under severe limits can prevent one outcome—withdrawal—while still leaving a larger conflict unresolved.

## 中文译文

**01** 1948年6月24日，从德国西部通往柏林西部各占领区的公路、铁路和运河运输被封锁。粮食和煤炭无法再沿常规路线运入。这座城市深处苏联占领区，因此问题不只是运输受阻：撤离会削弱西方盟国在德国的地位，而从地面强行打通道路可能引发战争。他们选择了第三条路：让飞机日复一日地越过封锁运送物资。空运真能维持整座城市人口的生活吗？

**02** 这场危机源于第二次世界大战结束后悬而未决的安排。1945年，美国、英国、法国和苏联分别占领德国的不同区域；虽然柏林位于苏联占领区内，这座城市也被分为四个占领区。各方就占领安排达成了协议，却没有就德国的长远未来取得一致。随着合作转为对立，西方政府着手联合重建其占领区，并推动建立西德国家。苏联领导人反对这一方向。由相互竞争的强国共同管理的柏林，成了各方计划碰撞的地点。

**03** 货币问题使争端迅速激化。1948年6月，西方盟国在其德国占领区推行新货币；随后柏林也出现了围绕货币的争夺。苏联当局试图将自己的货币改革推广到全市。此举失败后，他们于6月24日切断了通往柏林西部各区的陆路和水路。历史研究把货币改革视为直接导火索，但封锁属于一场更广泛的斗争：谁来决定德国的未来。封锁还截断了正常的电力和必需品供应，使西部各区面临的压力成为眼前的实际问题，而非仅具象征意义。

**04** 西方盟国仍可通过此前商定的空中走廊进入柏林。他们在柏林的驻军远少于附近的苏军，而派陆上车队强行通行可能导致武装对抗。空运提供了一种继续留在当地、又不立即用地面部队试探封锁的办法。这既是政治选择，也是一场后勤上的冒险：飞机能够援助柏林居民，但前提是航班足够密集，足以替代火车和驳船。6月26日，美国启动空运行动，英国的航班随后加入。开始时，任何一方都无法确定这场考验会持续多久。

**05** 运入几批应急物资尚可办到；持续数月为一座城市提供食物和取暖燃料则完全不同。飞机必须运输面粉、其他食品，尤其是煤炭；煤很重，却是发电和取暖所必需的。柏林的机场和卸货人员必须应对不断增多的航班。行动逐步扩大、效率也得到提高，并没有在最初展示坚守决心后就结束。即便如此，空运也无法恢复城市的正常生活。居民仍面临短缺，而随着冬天临近，稳定运入燃料与运入食物同样重要。

**06** 柏林居民并非只是政府间较量的旁观者。他们继续留在城中、在机场工作，并公开反对苏联施压，这些行动影响了空运的政治意义。9月，大批民众聚集在国会大厦附近，反对苏联支配柏林，并敦促西方国家留下。这场示威并没有独自决定危机的走向；持续的航班、更广泛的外交活动以及双方承受的压力同样重要。但它表明，保障西柏林的供应，也关系到当地居民的选择，而不仅取决于飞机和外交地图。

**07** 到1949年春，持续不断的物资运输使柏林西部各区迅速陷入崩溃的可能性降低。西方对苏联占领区的贸易限制也增加了那里的压力。四国同意解除各自的运输和贸易限制，苏联的封锁于1949年5月12日结束。空运持续到9月，以便储备物资，应对交通再次被切断的可能。封锁结束并未解决最初关于德国前途的争端。当月晚些时候，德意志联邦共和国在西部成立；随后东部也建立了另一个国家。

**08** 柏林空运之所以成功，是因为西方盟国把一个地理上的弱点转化为能够应对的实际难题，尽管这需要付出巨大的成本与努力。空中走廊使他们避开了地面交锋的直接危险，而持续的物资运输让坚守的决定变得可信。封锁没能迫使他们撤离，却加深了飞机和谈判都无法迅速消除的分裂。柏林仍是一座分裂的城市。这段历史表明，在严峻限制下作出的选择可以避免一种结果——撤离——却仍可能无法解决更大的冲突。

## 重点表达 · Key expressions

- **bring a dispute to a head**：使争端达到紧要关头
- **an immediate trigger**：直接导火索
- **a logistical gamble**：后勤上的冒险
- **a show of resolve**：展示坚守决心的行动
- **build up supplies**：储备物资

## 阅读理解 · Reading check

1. Why did Berlin's location make the blockade especially dangerous for the Western Allies?
2. Why was the airlift both a political choice and a logistical gamble?
3. What did the end of the blockade fail to resolve?

### 参考答案 · Answers

1. The western sectors lay inside the Soviet occupation zone, so normal land supply could be cut and a forced ground passage risked war.
2. It let the Allies stay without an immediate ground confrontation, but aircraft had to deliver enough food and fuel repeatedly to sustain the city.
3. It did not resolve the dispute over Germany's future; separate western and eastern German states followed.
